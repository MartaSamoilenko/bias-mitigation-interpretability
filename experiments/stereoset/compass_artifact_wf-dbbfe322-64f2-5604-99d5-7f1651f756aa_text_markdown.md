# Localization Methods for Gender Bias in LLMs: A Research Landscape for an ICLR-Level Comparison Paper

## TL;DR
- The strongest, most defensible framing for your paper is a **head-to-head faithfulness benchmark** of localization methods (adding EAP-IG-inputs, mask optimization/UGS, path patching, ACDC, and sparse-feature circuits to your existing DLA/DE/AP) evaluated with **causal, ablation-based metrics** — CPR/CMD and AUROC as in MIB — rather than the ranking-correlation-only view; your DLA-vs-AP Spearman gap (0.12–0.31) is consistent with the field's finding that DLA/probing are correlational and poorly track causal importance.
- The clearest gap you can fill: **no existing paper systematically compares localization methods *specifically for gender bias* using causal faithfulness metrics plus a downstream mitigation + capability-preservation evaluation.** Existing bias-MI papers each use one method (Vig causal mediation, Chintam ACDC, Adiga attention scaling, Chandna EAP, Marks sparse-feature circuits) and rarely benchmark methods against each other.
- Standard current practice: evaluate with **necessity (ablate circuit → behavior drops) and sufficiency (keep only circuit → behavior preserved)**, report **faithfulness/completeness/minimality**, use **counterfactual (resample) ablations**, and validate downstream mitigation on **StereoSet, CrowS-Pairs, BBQ, WinoBias/Winogender, BOLD** while checking capability preservation on general benchmarks and **TruthfulQA**.

## Key Findings

**1. The method space you should compare against.** Beyond DLA, DE, and Activation Patching, the localization methods most relevant and citable for a 2026 ICLR paper are: Edge Attribution Patching (EAP) and EAP-IG (integrated-gradients variant, the current SOTA attribution method); ACDC (search-based, the standard automated-circuit baseline); path patching (the gold-standard causal localization technique); differentiable/mask-optimization methods (Edge Pruning, DiscoGP, subnetwork probing, UGS); sparse-feature circuits (SAE-based, ICLR 2025, with a demonstrated gender-debiasing application via SHIFT); causal scrubbing (a hypothesis-testing/validation framework rather than a ranker); and the representation-level family — probing classifiers, concept erasure (LEACE, INLP, R-LACE), and DAS (Distributed Alignment Search). Newer 2025–2026 attribution refinements include EAP-GP, AtP*, and RelP.

**2. Metrics.** The field has moved decisively toward causal, intervention-based evaluation. The Mechanistic Interpretability Benchmark (MIB, ICML 2025) is the canonical reference and defines two size-integrated faithfulness metrics — CPR and CMD — plus AUROC against a known ground-truth circuit. Sufficiency and necessity are the two core causal tests; faithfulness/completeness/minimality are the standard circuit-quality triad. A crucial caveat: faithfulness scores are highly sensitive to ablation choice (zero/mean/resample), and rankings can reverse — so you must report multiple ablation types.

**3. Comparison-table conventions.** Recent top-venue tables put **methods as rows** and **(task × model) faithfulness metrics + compute cost as columns**, with a random baseline and often an exact-patching skyline. MIB is the template.

**4. Downstream evaluation.** Bias-mitigation papers report bias benchmarks (StereoSet, CrowS-Pairs, BBQ, WinoBias, Winogender, BOLD) alongside capability preservation (perplexity, GLUE-style tasks, commonsense reasoning, and TruthfulQA), and compare mitigation methods (full fine-tuning, LoRA, targeted DPO, targeted ablation/editing, concept erasure).

**5. The gap.** Bias-MI papers each apply one localization method; none benchmark localization methods against one another for bias with causal metrics and a mitigation study. That is your contribution.

## Details

### A. Localization / attribution methods to add to your comparison

**Edge Attribution Patching (EAP) and EAP-IG.** EAP (Syed et al., 2023) approximates the causal effect of corrupting each *edge* in the computation graph with a first-order Taylor expansion, requiring only two forward passes and one backward pass — O(1) in the number of edges, versus O(n) for exact activation/path patching. Vanilla EAP suffers from a saturation/zero-gradient problem (when a component is saturated, the local gradient is ~0 even if the component matters), which is acute with KL-divergence metrics. EAP-IG (Hanna et al., 2024) fixes this by integrating gradients along the straight-line path between corrupted and clean activations (Riemann sum of m steps, so m× the cost of EAP), yielding more faithful circuits with fewer edges. On MIB, **EAP-IG-inputs using counterfactual ablations generally performs best** on both the CMD and CPR metrics across GPT-2 Small, Qwen-2.5-0.5B, Gemma-2-2B, and Llama-3.1-8B. There are two IG variants — EAP-IG-inputs (interpolate the input embeddings) and EAP-IG-activations (interpolate the edge activations). This is the single most important method to add: it is the current attribution SOTA, cheap, and directly comparable to your AP.

**ACDC (Automatic Circuit Discovery)** (Conmy et al., NeurIPS 2023 Spotlight) automates the manual MI workflow: represent the model as a DAG, then iteratively prune edges via activation patching whenever removing them changes the output (KL divergence) less than a threshold. On the GPT-2-Small Greater-Than task it rediscovered 5/5 component types and **selected 68 of the 32,000 edges, all of which had been manually found by previous work** (note: the widely cited "68 of 32,000 edges" figure is the Greater-Than result, not IOI — for IOI, ACDC recovered a partial set of the known core heads). ACDC is the standard search-based baseline but is expensive (forward passes scale with graph size); "Attribution Patching Outperforms Automated Circuit Discovery" (Syed et al.) is the key result showing EAP is faster and often better than ACDC on ROC-AUC.

**Path patching** (Goldowsky-Dill et al., 2023; Wang et al., IOI, 2023) is the "most standard and widely-accepted" causal localization technique: cache clean and corrupted activations, then replace individual components' values along specific *paths* to see which maximally explain the logits. It is the gold-standard for validating a hypothesized circuit but is expensive. Include it as a "ground-truth-ish" reference against which cheaper methods are scored.

**Causal scrubbing** (Chan et al., Redwood, 2022) is not a ranking method but a *hypothesis-testing* framework: it tests whether a hypothesized computational structure is sufficient by resampling activations in every way the hypothesis says should not matter and checking behavior is preserved. Use it (or a lighter necessity/sufficiency ablation) to *validate* whichever circuit each method produces, not to rank components.

**Differentiable / mask-optimization methods.** Subnetwork Probing (Cao, Sanh & Rush, 2021) learns a continuous (hard-concrete) mask over components. Edge Pruning (Bhaskar et al., 2024) and DiscoGP (Yu et al., 2025) formulate circuit discovery as differentiable optimization over computation-graph masks. DiffMask (De Cao et al., EMNLP 2020) learns to mask inputs/hidden states while staying differentiable. On MIB, **mask optimization (UGS) is competitive with the best attribution methods on circuit localization** (though memory-heavy). These are worth including as a distinct methodological family.

**Sparse feature circuits** (Marks et al., ICLR 2025) use SAEs to build circuits over human-interpretable *features* (not polysemantic heads/neurons), scoring features via attribution patching / integrated gradients, with an explicit SAE-error node. Critically for you, their **SHIFT** method debiases a classifier by ablating human-identified spurious (gender) features in the worst case where gender perfectly predicts profession (Bias-in-Bios) — a direct bias-localization-and-mitigation precedent. Caveat: MIB found SAE features are **not** better than raw neurons/hidden dimensions for causal variable localization.

**Representation-level methods (probing, concept erasure, DAS).** Linear probes detect *whether* a bias direction is present but are correlational: amnesic probing (Elazar et al., 2021) and MIB both show probing accuracy does not correlate with causal importance — the same phenomenon behind your low DLA–AP Spearman. Concept erasure removes a concept subspace: INLP (iterative nullspace projection), R-LACE (adversarial, rank-1 gender removal), and LEACE (closed-form, provably prevents all linear classifiers from recovering the concept while minimally changing representations; demonstrated on gender-bias reduction in BERT via "concept scrubbing"). DAS (Distributed Alignment Search) is the supervised causal-variable method that **won the MIB causal-variable track**, beating SAEs and neurons. These belong in a separate "representation/direction" track from the circuit/component track.

**Newer attribution refinements (2025–2026)** you should at least cite:
- **EAP-GP** (Zhang et al., "EAP-GP: Mitigating Saturation Effect in Gradient-based Automated Circuit Identification," arXiv:2502.06852; NeurIPS 2025): uses adaptive gradient paths and reports normalized-faithfulness improvements of **up to 17.7% over EAP-IG** on GPT-2 Small/Medium/XL over six tasks (e.g., IOI at 97.5% sparsity: EAP-GP 80.1% vs EAP-IG 62.4% vs EAP 56.9%).
- **AtP\*** (Kramár et al., 2024): an improved attribution-patching variant addressing gradient noise/cancellation.
- **RelP** (Jafari et al., "RelP: Faithful and Efficient Circuit Discovery via Relevance Patching," arXiv:2508.21258; NeurIPS 2025 MechInterp Workshop Spotlight): relevance patching that dramatically improves Pearson correlation with exact activation patching at the same cost as attribution patching — for MLP outputs in GPT-2 Large, **attribution patching achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.006, whereas RelP reaches 0.956**.

### B. Standard experiments and metrics in method-comparison papers

**Ground-truth baselines.** Because real-model circuits have no known ground truth, papers use: (i) synthetic/compiled models with known circuits (InterpBench, Tracr, TracrBench), where AUROC over edges is computable; (ii) exact activation/path patching as an expensive skyline that cheaper methods approximate (report Pearson/Spearman correlation to exact indirect effect — this is exactly your DLA-vs-AP setup, generalized); and (iii) previously hand-discovered circuits (IOI, Greater-Than) as reference.

**Faithfulness metrics beyond NMAE and Spearman.** The MIB pair is now canonical: **CPR (integrated Circuit Performance Ratio)**, the area under the faithfulness-vs-size curve, higher is better, rewarding components with positive task effect; and **CMD (integrated Circuit–Model Distance)**, the area between the faithfulness curve and f=1, lower is better, rewarding components with *any* strong effect (positive or negative). Both marginalize over circuit size (evaluated at ~10 sizes — k ∈ {.001, .002, .005, .01, .02, .05, .1, .2, .5, 1} — via a trapezoidal Riemann sum), so they jointly capture faithfulness and minimality. Base faithfulness is f(C) = (m(C) − m(∅)) / (m(N) − m(∅)) with m = logit difference, ∅ = fully ablated model, N = full network. Circuit size uses a weighted edge count normalized to a percentage of possible edges. Other common faithfulness families: insertion/deletion curves, comprehensiveness/sufficiency (DeYoung et al., 2020), AOPC, and ROC-AUC against ground-truth edges.

**Sufficiency vs. necessity.** Necessity: ablating the circuit from the model removes the target behavior. Sufficiency: keeping only the circuit (ablating its complement) preserves the behavior. Report both; they are the operationalization of "are these the right components."

**Completeness and minimality.** Faithfulness — circuit replicates full-model behavior; completeness — circuit contains all components the model uses (no important component omitted); minimality — no proper subset also suffices (test by ablating within-circuit components, often incrementally adding heads in importance order and measuring recovered AUC). These come from Wang et al.'s IOI paper and are the standard triad.

**The robustness caveat you must address.** Miller, Chughtai & Saunders (COLM 2024), "Transformer Circuit Faithfulness Metrics Are Not Robust," show faithfulness scores are highly sensitive to seemingly minor ablation choices (zero vs mean vs resample; token-position handling) and that **method rankings can reverse** depending on the ablation. Practical consequence for your paper: report results under multiple ablation types (at minimum mean and counterfactual/resample), and prefer counterfactual/resample ablations, which MIB argues keep components on-distribution. MIB itself found counterfactual ablations beat mean and optimal ablations.

### C. What comparison tables look like

The MIB circuit-localization table is the template: **rows = methods** (Random control; exact edge activation patching EActP; node/edge attribution NAP/EAP; EAP-IG-inputs, EAP-IG-activations, NAP-IG; Information Flow Routes; mask optimization UGS — optionally ACDC), and **columns = metrics per (task × model)**: CMD and CPR for each of IOI, Arithmetic, MCQA, ARC across GPT-2-Small, Qwen-2.5-0.5B, Gemma-2-2B, Llama-3.1-8B, plus an InterpBench AUROC column. Bold marks the best per column; a random baseline anchors the bottom. Representative MIB CMD values (lower is better): on IOI, EAP-IG variants reach ~0.01–0.03 vs Random ~0.69–0.75; InterpBench AUROC (higher is better) tops out at 0.81 (EAP-IG-activations) vs 0.44 random. **Compute cost** is reported as forward/backward pass counts and asymptotic complexity (O(1) for EAP, O(m) for EAP-IG, O(n) for exact patching/ACDC), plus wall-clock runtime and peak memory (UGS is flagged as memory-heavy, EAP-IG-activations as slow). For your paper, add a compute column (passes, wall-clock, memory) and a stability column (variance across seeds / perturbations), since Chandna et al. show bias edges are unstable.

### D. Downstream evaluation after bias localization + mitigation

**Bias benchmarks.** StereoSet (your current data; intra- and inter-sentence, Stereotype Score / LMS / ICAT), CrowS-Pairs (**1,508 minimally different sentence pairs across nine bias types — race, gender/gender identity, sexual orientation, religion, age, nationality, disability, physical appearance, socioeconomic status**; % stereotype ideally 0.5), BBQ (QA format, ambiguous vs disambiguated, 9–11 categories), WinoBias and Winogender (coreference-based occupational gender bias), and BOLD (open-ended generation bias). For gender specifically, WinoBias/Winogender plus StereoSet-gender and CrowS-gender are the core set.

**Capability preservation.** Standard practice measures the accuracy/perplexity gap between the base and debiased model on general tasks — commonsense (COPA/Balanced COPA, OpenBookQA, ARC-E/C, BoolQ), language-modeling perplexity, GLUE-style understanding — and reports **TruthfulQA** for truthfulness preservation (e.g., BiasFreeBench reports BoolQ, COPA, and TruthfulQA deltas and finds DPO/SFT preserve capability while task-vector editing degrades it). ATLAS (Adiga, Nushi & Chandrasekaran, ACL 2025, tested on BBQ, CrowS-Pairs, and WinoGender with GPT-2 XL, GPT-J 6B, LLaMA-2 7B, and LLaMA-3 8B) reports "an average increase of only 0.34% in perplexity when the intervention is applied" and "an average improvement of 0.28 points in the bias score across all the datasets." Sparse-feature-circuit ablation and neuron editing (Interpret-ME; Yu & Ananiadou's gender-neuron editing) report bias reduction with minimal general-performance loss.

**Mitigation methods to compare.** Full fine-tuning; LoRA (and fairness-specific variants FairLoRA, BA-LoRA); targeted DPO on preference pairs; targeted ablation/editing of localized components (circuit breaking, Interpret-ME neuron editing, ATLAS attention scaling); and concept erasure (LEACE/INLP) as a representation-level baseline. The natural experimental spine: localize with each method → apply the *same* mitigation to each method's components → measure bias reduction vs capability retention → the method whose components yield the best bias/capability trade-off is the "best localizer" by a downstream criterion (complementing the intrinsic CPR/CMD/AUROC scores).

### E. Recent bias + MI papers (2024–2026) and the gap

- **Vig et al. (2020)** — causal mediation analysis pinpointing gender-bias neurons/heads in GPT-2; the founding template.
- **Chintam et al. (2023)** — automated circuit discovery + targeted ablation/fine-tuning of late-layer attention heads to reduce gender bias with minimal performance loss.
- **"Interpreting Bias in LLMs: A Feature-Based Approach" (2024)** — activation + attribution patching for gender bias on LLaMA-2-7B, LLaMA-3-8B, Mistral-7B with counterfactual datasets, targeting both bias origin and propagation.
- **ATLAS / "Attention Speaks Volumes" (Adiga et al., ACL 2025)** — localizes bias to later layers (last third) via attention scores, mitigates by scaling attention; ~0.34% perplexity cost, ~0.28-point average bias-score improvement across BBQ/CrowS-Pairs/WinoGender and four models.
- **Interpret-ME / "Understanding and Mitigating Gender Bias via Interpretable Model Editing" (ICLR 2025 submission)** and **Yu & Ananiadou (2025)** neuron-level "gender neurons" editing — reduce bias while preserving abilities without large datasets or fine-tuning.
- **Sparse Feature Circuits + SHIFT (Marks et al., ICLR 2025)** — SAE-feature circuits with a gender-debiasing application.
- **"Dissecting Bias in LLMs" (Chandna, Bashir & Sen, 2025)** — uses EAP on GPT-2-Small/Large and Llama-2 for demographic and gender bias; key findings: bias is highly localized to a few layers/edges, but the identified edges are **unstable** under lexical, syntactic, and fine-tuning perturbations, and ablating bias edges **degrades unrelated NLP tasks (NER, linguistic acceptability)** due to component sharing.
- **"Locate, Steer, and Improve" (2026 survey of actionable MI)** and **"Mechanics of Bias and Reasoning: Chain-of-Thought and gender bias" (2026)** contextualize the subfield.

**The gap:** every one of these fixes a *single* localization method. None asks *which localization method best identifies bias-causing components*, using causal faithfulness metrics, across multiple models, with a downstream mitigation + capability-preservation study — and none confronts the DLA-vs-causal-methods discrepancy you already found. That is precisely the contribution space for your paper.

## Recommendations

1. **Reframe the core claim around causal faithfulness, not ranking correlation.** Your DLA-vs-AP Spearman of 0.12–0.31 is a *finding*, not a flaw: it corroborates that DLA (a direct, correlational readout) diverges from causal importance, mirroring amnesic-probing and MIB results. Lead with CPR/CMD-style faithfulness and treat rank correlation as secondary.

2. **Add methods in this priority order:** (i) **EAP-IG-inputs** — cheap, SOTA, directly comparable to AP (highest ROI); (ii) **mask optimization** (Edge Pruning or UGS) — a distinct, competitive family; (iii) **path patching** as an expensive causal reference/skyline; (iv) **ACDC** as the search-based baseline; (v) **sparse-feature circuits + a representation-level method (LEACE or DAS)** to span the feature/direction axis. Keep DLA and DE as the "cheap/correlational" end of the spectrum. Optionally cite/benchmark EAP-GP and RelP as recent SOTA refinements.

3. **Adopt MIB-style metrics and table layout.** Report CPR and CMD (integrated over ~10 circuit sizes), plus necessity and sufficiency ablations, under at least two ablation types (mean and counterfactual/resample) to preempt the Miller et al. robustness critique. Add compute (passes, wall-clock, memory) and stability (across seeds and StereoSet-perturbations) columns — stability is especially salient given Chandna et al.'s instability finding.

4. **Build a downstream mitigation bridge.** For each localization method, ablate/edit its top components (matched budget), then evaluate bias reduction on StereoSet + CrowS-Pairs + WinoBias/Winogender + BBQ and capability retention on perplexity + commonsense + **TruthfulQA**. Compare against full-FT, LoRA, and targeted-DPO mitigation baselines. This makes "best localizer" an actionable, not just intrinsic, claim.

5. **Explicitly test the entanglement question.** Following Chandna et al., measure whether ablating bias components harms NER/linguistic-acceptability/general tasks — quantifying the bias/capability trade-off per method is a strong, novel result.

**Thresholds that change the recommendation:** if EAP-IG's correlation to exact patching on your bias task is low (Pearson < 0.5, as RelP showed can happen for MLP/residual components — 0.006 for GPT-2-Large MLP outputs), prefer path patching or RelP and report the failure. If mask-optimization memory is infeasible at Gemma-2-2B/Llama scale, drop it to an appendix. If SAE features underperform neurons on your bias task (as MIB found generally), report sparse-feature circuits as a negative result rather than a headline method.

## Caveats

- **Faithfulness is ablation-dependent.** Miller et al. (COLM 2024) show rankings can reverse with the ablation method; any single-ablation result is fragile. Report multiple ablations.
- **Bias metrics are not standardized.** There is no consensus bias metric; StereoSet, CrowS-Pairs, and BBQ can disagree, and several (StereoSet, CrowS-Pairs) have documented construct-validity criticisms. Use a revised CrowS-Pairs and report multiple benchmarks.
- **Probing ≠ causation.** Probes and DLA reveal presence, not use; do not draw causal conclusions from them without intervention (this is central to your paper's logic).
- **Bias components may be unstable and entangled.** Chandna et al. find bias edges shift under perturbation and overlap with general-competence components — so "localization" may be less crisp for bias than for IOI/Greater-Than, which is itself a reportable result.
- **Some cited items are preprints/workshop or forum posts** (causal scrubbing is a Redwood/AlignmentForum writeup; several 2025–2026 arXiv items — EAP-GP, RelP, the actionable-MI survey — are recent and some are workshop-tier rather than main-conference peer-reviewed). Weight them accordingly.
- **MIB tasks are not bias tasks.** MIB's leaderboard covers IOI/Arithmetic/MCQA/ARC, not bias; you are adapting its methodology to a new domain, which is part of the novelty but means you cannot directly cite MIB numbers as bias results.
